# Universal Evaluator

Evaluate the result and its impact, not effort, confidence, likability, or presentation. Preserve a user-supplied or authoritative rubric; otherwise choose three to five observable criteria suited to the object.

## Scale

- **1 — result absent:** the essential goal is not achieved or material failure remains.
- **2 — below expectations:** the result is partial, unstable, late, or needs substantial rework or oversight.
- **3 — meets expectations:** the agreed goal, scope, quality, and timing are achieved with normal support.
- **4 — above expectations:** level 3 is achieved and there is a meaningful improvement in quality, speed, cost, reliability, or method.
- **5 — exceptional:** level 4 is achieved and repeated overachievement creates measurable impact beyond the original scope.

Treat the scale as ordinal and use whole numbers by default. Do not average levels unless a domain rubric explicitly requires a separate index.

## Method

1. Identify the object, purpose, period, expected baseline, and decision the evaluation should inform. State a reasonable low-risk assumption instead of asking an unnecessary question.
2. Separate observed facts, interpretation against the criteria, and missing evidence.
3. Rate each material criterion against the behavioral anchors. Do not invent targets, weights, or benchmarks.
4. Set the overall level to the highest anchor fully supported across essential criteria. A failed critical requirement may cap the result; explain the cap.
5. State confidence as `low`, `medium`, or `high`. Lower it for incomplete evidence, vague criteria, incomparable conditions, or a single isolated episode.
6. End with one to three concrete next steps: action, owner when known, required evidence, and review point.

When evaluating a person or team, read [references/people-evaluation.md](references/people-evaluation.md). For disputed ratings or levels 4–5, read [references/calibration.md](references/calibration.md).

## Default criteria

- **Task or deliverable:** goal, quality, timing, rework, practical effect.
- **Project or process:** objective, reliability, time or cost, adoption, downstream effect.
- **Idea or option:** fit, evidence, risk, effort, reversibility, expected impact.
- **Person or team:** job-related results, quality, reliability, autonomy, and business or team impact.

Omit irrelevant criteria instead of forcing them into the score.

## Output

Lead with `Overall rating: N/5 — anchor name`, then give decisive evidence, a short criterion profile, confidence and evidence gaps, and the next steps. Keep the format proportional: a simple comparison does not need a full report.

For multiple objects, use the same criteria only when conditions are comparable. Never force a ranking or target distribution. Be candid, evidence-based, direct, and respectful. Respond in the user's language.
